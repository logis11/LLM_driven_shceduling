# Harness changelog

The harness tree's own record of what its committed data files and programs changed, newest first. The scoring spec (`scoring/scoring-spec.yaml`) and the guard spec (`guards/guard-spec.yaml`) are frozen here (Phase 8 spec, decision 19): a change to either after its freeze is an entry below before any run reads it, and the RQ0 gate spec (`experiments/rq0-gate.yaml`) pins the frozen bytes by hash. The metrics doc and the data contracts keep their own changelogs.

## 2026-10-09 — the consumers reworked on the rebuilt dataset; the scoring spec re-frozen, the guard spec 1.1, the RQ0 gate spec re-pinned (jioh 9.14)

- **Scoring spec re-frozen** as of this commit: 49 files, 75 terms (78 before). Gone: the three `compositor` terms and the two meeting `video` terms, which the rebuilt entries no longer carry; in: `c2-p2a-idle`'s two terms with `base: c2-p2a`; `c2-p3a`'s render term reads turnaround at 0.5 in place of progress. The seven windowed terms run from 60 s to each file's end, and a lint rule requires every window's two bounds to coincide with ground-truth segment boundaries of the compiled file. Every interaction-latency term names its channel: `input` on 25, `timer` on Kdenlive's and GIMP's fourteen (mouse-driven operations carried as the applications' own timer components; no keystroke stream exists for them) and on `c7-mail`'s one (its 26.385 s cut holds no keystroke; the term reports, with no base). The header states the no-headroom rules per term. Pinned by SHA-256 in the RQ0 gate spec.
- **Guard spec 1.0 → 1.1**: `c2_pair` compares the two runs' configuration sequences over the segment whose ground truth differs — the entry in force at its start and every entry stamped inside it, as (algorithm, params, cap) — failing identical sequences under `oracle` and the recognition conditions, not applicable under `fixed` and `random`; the `fixed_identical` flag retired. The pair list's lint reads the recipes' label-changing `c2-` variants, so `c2-p2a-idle` is not a pair and the list stays at three. The `whitelist` condition is two in every frozen list — `shipped_catalogue` and `strongest_name_table` — in the evaluator's tuple, the five schema enums (records, aggregates, scores, grades, experiment spec), the two guard schemas, the guard spec's `applies_to` lists and metrics §5. 9.11's three changes carried: the 30 s starvation floor (the executor's watchdog bound), the provenance-share grounding, P1's flag; the grounding-kind labels of 9.11 D29.
- **RQ0 gate spec**, `experiments/rq0-gate.yaml`: the judging set 25 → 19 and K 13 → 10 (the existing majority reading). Out: the six files holding idle-class work (`c1-indexing`, `c7-indexing`, `c2-p1b`, `c1-backup`, `c7-backup`, `c2-p3b`) — the executor's idle rule places the background task below every row and the cap is work-conserving, so no row reaches it; `c7-mail` — its only term reads nothing. In again: `c7-meeting` — the measured call's cycle runs consume the boot slice in about a fifth of its cycles, so the tie with the boot default that moved it out in 8.8 no longer holds. `c7-media` stays reporting-only (a 32 ms worst case against a 33.3 ms period, a 1.3 ms margin); `c2-p2a`, `c2-p2b` and `c7-gaming` stay with their caveat restated to the deadline-class chain. The boot-default sweep has ten points (14 000 µs added above the voice job's 12.2 ms ninth-decile cut; every reason rewritten on the rebuilt files' steps; `boot-defaults/ostep-slice-14000us.json`). The reporting lines: `variant_set` ×8 (the venue factor ×0.8 and ×1.25, the chain ×1.2 and ×1.4, the C2 pairs' first segment at 30 s and 120 s, the carried half-widths' two corners), `trace_replay` on four files at tolerance 0.17, `input_wake_count`, `longest_wait` (30 s floor, 15 s reopen), `bounding_pair` on `c2-p2a`/`c2-p2a-idle`; the statements `judging-set`, `idle-class-files`, `executor-assumptions`, `simulator-conformance`, `prior-table-structure`, `k-grounding`, `n-grounding`, `boot-default-sweep`, `sensitivity-lines`, `re-measurement-contingency`, `layer1-interval`, `failure-procedure`; `files.variants` lists the 78 rebuilt judging files the sensitivity lines read, run and reported, never judged.
- **Records under the ratified contract**: the `job` primitive consumes the latest grid tick at or before each `ready(cause = timer_tick)` line, the grid anchored at the head's first such line (t₀ off the trace); skipped ticks are `job` rows with no completion, counted as missed — the records' twenty-fourth column, `skipped`; the chain guard counts consumed ticks; the `deadline` cross-check. The `mock-media` fixture re-derived by hand (`tools/tests/fixtures/mock-media/worked.md`). The mock daemon's projection carries `declared_class`. metrics.md §5, §6.2, §11, §13.
- **The sensitivity variants** (9.14 decision 12): `dataset/variants.yaml` names nine sets over the judging files — four scaling sets on the blessed artifacts, two segment-length and two carried-bounds sets recompiled with the pinned library, and the trace-replay set on one pooled repeat per entry (`dataset/replay/`, six streams from the raw releases). The 78 artifacts and their manifest, `dataset/build.variants.manifest.json`, are pinned beside the main one.
- **The runner**: a variant file is scored on its base's terms, each window moved to the variant's own segments by index (`scoring.with_variants`; the segment-length sets need it) — before this the scorer skipped a variant id, having no entry for it, and every sensitivity line would have read an empty gap. Each run is aggregated as soon as its records are built and its rows dropped: on the rebuilt set the smoke's 196 runs hold 43 million record rows, `c6-dual`'s 14.5 million each, and holding them all until the end swapped a 16 GB machine for eleven hours; outputs unchanged. The hog test and the CPU-since reading of the boost-window rule (§6.9, §6.10) find their entries by bisection over the trace-ordered lists instead of scanning them: a `random` schedule that switches into MLFQ asks them at every 100 ms boost instant, and on `c6-dual` the scans made one run cost over five hours; outputs unchanged.
