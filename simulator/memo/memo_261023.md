# Part B — conformance (a) with Q2, as stage X

## B1. What changed

- **A ready line per completed blocking primitive** (metrics §11 item 1; trace-clarifications memo §4). A WAIT met from a mailbox and a TIMER in backlog now write their line at the instant they complete; inside the holder's occupancy the harness pairs it with a wait of zero. The arrival step writes `ready(arrive)` first and then the zero-wait lines of what it passed — tick 0 of a periodic task, a WAIT met by a wake stored before the arrival — the order the mocks and the hand fixtures use. A task woken from a block writes its own cause first, then the zero-wait lines of what the step passes.
- **RUN→RUN is one occupancy** (memo_261010 Part C). `advance()` is gone; `boundary()` steps the holder on its lane with no requeue and no pick and writes `x_run_cont`. A boundary still ends the occupancy when the program blocks or exits. After a continuing boundary the core asks the policy whether any runnable task now preempts the holder, since the step may have woken or spawned one, or moved the holder's own EDF deadline.
- **Q2** (decided 2026-10-16). A slice that expires while the policy picks the same holder again is no switch: no `run_end`/`run_start` pair. A drain waiting on that slice end applies first and the incoming algorithm picks (`mock-switch` at 12000: the hog's slice ends, FIFO applies, the hog keeps the lane with no `run_end`).
- **One place for the boundary.** A preemption, a cap park, a config apply or a policy timer arriving at the very µs a RUN ends leaves the boundary to that RUN's own Lane event. Before X each of the four called `advance()` itself, and the preemption path could write `run_end(preempt)` and then `run_end(block)` for the same occupancy. [confirmed, from the code]
- **Fixed beside it:** a segment-bound task that EXITs before its depart wrote a second `task_end` at the depart (66 of 128 local runs). The harness reads the later one, so a task that finished was counted as not completed. The lane lint found it; before X it was buried under the RUN→RUN violations. [confirmed]

## B2. Readings X took that the documents do not settle (to confirm with 인지오, Part D)

1. A FORK that finds a slot writes no `ready(fork_slot)`: the fork-slot wait is the block, and there was none. [inferred from the wording "the fork-slot wait"]
2. A drain applying at the end of a RUN inside an occupancy (data contracts §7, **Drain**: "when that task's slice or current RUN ends") treats the holder as freshly dispatched by the incoming algorithm, as a switch out of FIFO does (rule a), instead of handing the lane back through the ready set. Before X the lane was released at every RUN end, so the question did not arise.
3. memo_261010 Part C proposed as the positive control that the `x_run_cont` count equal the old count of `ready(arrive)` lines from `advance()`. **It does not hold**: `c1-gaming` under boot MLFQ 52,547 against 35,809, `c2-p2b` 79,853 against 43,101. The schedule itself changes — a holder that keeps the lane lets wakes pile into its mailbox, so more boundaries pass without a block — and the equality assumed it does not. That proposal was mine and was inferred, not measured. The conservation checks of B3 replace it. [confirmed]
