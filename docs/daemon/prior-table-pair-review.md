Decision record: `_dev/research/jioh/task-9.14-consumer-rework/changelog.md`.

The pre-execution check on `daemon/driver-table/prior.yaml`: for every pair of rows the experiment will hold against each other, one written judgement that names the knob that differs, the scored term of the exercising file that knob moves, and that the direction agrees with the scoring weights in `harness/scoring/scoring-spec.yaml`. The lint already refuses a same-mode pair whose default configurations are byte-identical; this document is the judgement on top of that. It is a manipulation check, not a tuning result: it shows the rows differ in the intended direction, not that they are the best rows. How much of the available spread the table captures is RQ5's empirical check after the RQ0 gate.

Three lists, per the Phase 6 and Phase 7 specs. The 16 same-mode pairs (`wanted=true` against `wanted=false`), the three C2 row-pairs as the judging files actually exercise them — only one of those is a same-mode pair — and the 16 C7 pairs. Rows no coreset file exercises are reviewed too, because the `random` condition can land on any row.

Reading the table's rules once (they are in the file's header): the mode picks the algorithm family and the attribute picks the cap; a wanted row's cap is the batch term's share of the file's scoring weight, an unwanted row's cap is the schema floor 0.05; params are the boot values throughout, so no pair differs by a param. Two executor rules sit below every row and decide several readings here: the cap is work-conserving and reaches a task only while it is in the behavioural batch class, and a task whose declared class is idle (the Tracker indexer, the Déjà Dup backup) runs only when no other-class task is runnable, so no row's share or cap reaches it. Demand is read from the manifest per file and, since 9.14, per segment.

## Findings

1. **The cap does not reach a background task the lane has nothing left for.** *2026-09-09:* `c2-p2a`'s download (then `network-bulk`, about 1 ms of run per 5.7 ms) never consumed a full slice, so by the batch-class rule it was not batch class and the `gaming/true` row's 0.333 cap was inert on it. *2026-10-09:* the measured download (SteamCMD's fresh install, 9.7) runs back to back with near-zero sleeps and is batch class, so the cap reaches it — but the chain, in EDF's deadline class, takes 0.95 of the lane, and what EDF leaves is below any cap, so the download's progress is expected no headroom for a different reason (scoring spec). The earlier conclusion that a cap on a behaviourally inferred class protects against unwanted CPU work but cannot favour wanted work holds, now because the deadline class leaves nothing to favour. p2 tests the attribute in neither direction (finding 5).
2. **Fourteen of the 32 cells have no coreset instance** (2026-09-09; closed by Phase 7, finding 4). Kept as the record of why the C7 counterparts exist.
3. **No pair failed the sentence test.** Every pair differs on a knob a sentence can name, and the direction agrees with the weights in every case. `prior.yaml` is unchanged in value by this review and its predecessors.
4. **All 32 cells are instanced (Phase 7).** The grid tool has the attribute axis and CI fails on an empty cell. Of the 66 segments on the grid (the bounding variant `c2-p2a-idle` included), 18 are `false`, so an always-`true` answer scores about 73 percent by segment count; the headline attribute metric is balanced accuracy with the confusion matrix beside it, raw accuracy only next to its majority baseline (the RQ0 gate spec's Layer-1 lines). Five segments are pre-committed misses on the attribute side — `indexing/true` and the `ml-train`, `render`, `transcode`, `backup` `false` cells — annotated `pre_committed_miss` and reported outside accuracy by the same rule as C6. Their rows are still measured under `oracle`.
5. **On every EDF row the cap does not reach the scored term: the periodic modes are measured on mode recognition alone.** *2026-09-09:* `meeting` and `media` scored TIMER consumers in the deadline class, which EDF serves before the residual class, so their `true`/`false` distance was unmeasured on the cap axis; `gaming` was held outside this family because the chain's woken stages were read as residual class, "which is why `c2-p2b` measures its cap". *2026-10-09:* the recognition vocabulary ratified on 2026-10-06 that the deadline class holds the whole frame chain, each stage with the frame's deadline, as the batch-class memo's rule B2 had it and as the simulator implements it; 인경민 measured `c2-p2b` at 0 of 7,199 frames missed under EDF whatever the cap. So gaming joins the family: on `c2-p2a`, `c2-p2b` and `c7-gaming` the two gaming rows tie on the chain term by design. What the gaming and meeting files still measure is the EDF rows against the boot MLFQ, whose 100 ms boost hands a residual hog a 10 ms slice inside a frame or a call cycle: on the gaming files the frame's slack is 0.8 ms (the pair, its chain at 0.95 of the lane) and 1.7 ms (the base, at 0.9), so the frame misses; on `c7-meeting` the measured call's cycle run consumes the boot slice in about a fifth of its cycles and is demoted into the upgrade's queue. That is mode recognition, the gap the RQ0 gate spec reads on these files with the caveat stated. The 2026-09-11 addendum's knife edge — a 10 ms residual slice exactly equal to the video consumer's tolerance, two executor rules deciding — is gone with the archetypes it was computed on: the meeting file is one task with a 10 ms audio-frame job, the media files' tolerances are 17 and 47 ms. `c7-media` stays no headroom against fixed on the measured tables (a 32 ms worst case against a 33.3 ms period, a 1.3 ms margin) and is reporting-only; `c7-meeting`, no longer tied with fixed, re-entered the judging set (9.14 decision 2).
6. **No C7 pair failed the sentence test** (2026-09-10). `prior.yaml` changed in two places then: `indexing/true`'s cap derived from `c1-indexing`'s weights (same value), and the thirteen `false` rows naming their C7 file.
7. **The idle class takes the indexing and backup pairs off the attribute axis.** New, 2026-10-09. Tracker's indexer and Déjà Dup's backup declare `SCHED_IDLE` on themselves on the depicted desktop (9.11 D4), the dataset carries the class, and the executor runs an idle task only when no other-class task is runnable, under every algorithm (9.11 D5; 9.13). On `c1-indexing`, `c7-indexing`, `c2-p1b`, `c1-backup`, `c7-backup` and `c2-p3b` no row's share or cap reaches the background task: its turnaround or progress is identical under every row and the editor's wait is untouched by it. The rows stand by the header rule and are correct in direction, and the six files are reporting-only (9.14 decision 2), their prediction stated. The depicted desktop differs for the indexer, which runs in `background.slice` at about 23 % of the CPU against the editor's slice (9.11 D16): the simulated indexer interferes less than the real one, so these files understate the headroom a real desktop would show. For the backup, which shares the editor's task group, the simulation matches the desktop.
8. **`c7-mail`'s cut holds no keystroke.** New, 2026-10-09. `c1-mail`'s 35 keystrokes, the SWELL Outlook stream's one typing burst in the replayed slice, fall between 45.9 s and 53.1 s; the counterpart is the base's first 26.385 s, the upgrade's CPU total (9.10 D41), so its mailer has 5,308 of its own timer wakes and no input wake. A judging file whose only term reads nothing cannot judge: `c7-mail` is reporting-only, its term read on the mailer's own wakes, and the mail mode's attribute is unmeasured (9.14 decision 2 as amended). Not taken: re-cutting the counterpart, a dataset change and a replay-offset op the compiler lacks.
9. **What the editors' terms read.** New, 2026-10-09. The measured editors (9.5) cost a keystroke about 5 ms (the office writer, the mail client), next to nothing (the browser) or about 78 ms (VS Code); the first three block inside one 10 ms slice and stay on top under MLFQ, VS Code is demoted within the keystroke and the boost returns it, so on the dev, compile, ml-train and indexing files the editor's keystroke waits read the floor cap's effect in the queue the editor and the batch job then share. Kdenlive and GIMP carry no keystroke stream — their measured driven phases are mouse-driven operations on their own timer components — so their terms read the wait of their own wakes, the application's responsiveness rather than a keystroke's echo (scoring spec). Every term of this review's rows is read on the channel the scoring spec names.
10. **The counterparts' P99s rest on few keystrokes.** New, 2026-10-09. A 26 s cut of a typing stream at about six keystrokes a second holds a hundred-odd input wakes (`c7-browsing` 29, `c7-office` 103, `c7-dev` 78, `c7-indexing` 30), and `c3-workday`'s browser has 7 in its one focused minute; a P99 over so few is the top one or two waits. The gate spec prints the count each P99 rests on beside the file (its input-wake-count line); the pair comparison is on the same wakes.

Stated limitation: the workloads, the scoring weights, the table, and this review have one author, and no second reader is planned. The empirical check after the gate (RQ5's configuration search) is what tests the table against something the author did not write.
