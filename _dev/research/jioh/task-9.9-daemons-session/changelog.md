## D1 — `system-daemon` split into system services and session processes (2026-09-22)

By 인지오's decision, scope-card item 16 (with items 1–15 depending on it): `system-daemon` describes the system services only — `systemd`, `dbus-daemon` and the other always-on services of a systemd-based system — and the session processes bound to it today, the compositor and shell (`gnome-shell`), the display server (`Xorg`) and the audio server (`pipewire`), leave it for an archetype or archetypes of their own, each grounded on its own observation under phase decision 2 — the form 9.8 D1 gave `electron-comms` (one entry per bound program, each on its own observation) and 9.6 D12 gave `tracker`'s full rescan (out of `cpu-batch` into an entry of its own). Grounds: the two populations differ in every class searched. By mechanism from source — the system services wake on fixed timers or on events (cron 60 s, multipathd 5 s, udisksd 600 s, the buses on traffic only; S2-01…S2-10, S2-21), while Mutter's per-view frame clock returns to IDLE after presentation with nothing scheduled and is inhibited while the output is off (S2-12), KWin arms its composite timer only on repaint requests (S2-15), Xorg has no idle timer of its own (S2-16), and PipeWire's graph stops ticking once WirePlumber suspends an idle node after 5 s (S2-18, S2-19). By observation — a PowerTOP report of an idle Debian 8 machine at its greeter gives the services 0.05–8.2 wakeups/s at 3.5 µs/s–1.2 ms/s each (S3-29); on a GNOME 49 Wayland desktop the audio server, journal, udev, logind and NetworkManager take no sample of 1.2 × 10⁶ cycles in 20 s while `gnome-shell` holds 47–74 % of all samples (S3-26); `gnome-shell` wakes about 1 500 times a second at a fresh login on Mutter 49.3 (S3-06); the desktop PowerTOP tables found put the compositor and the X server at the top and no system service among their quoted rows (S3-03, S3-13, S3-14, S3-28; S3 §3 T1). By treatment — the ananicy catalogue files the display and audio servers under `LowLatency_RT` at nice −12 and the buses under `Service` at nice 10 (S2-26). The present entry's values are 51 per-process means of eleven server-side services of a headless VM (scope card item 1), so every session-process binding inherits a population it is not part of — memo B6: "none of the three session processes was measured … the binding is not marked as an approximation anywhere". Not taken: one near-idle class with the session processes carried under a stated approximation, which the magnitudes above contradict by orders of magnitude in wake rate and the mechanisms by kind; narrowing the entry to the system services with the session processes left unbound until observed, when a runner can host a headless session (gnome-shell `--headless --virtual-monitor`, `kwin_wayland --virtual`, sway headless, Xvfb or the dummy Xorg driver, PipeWire with a null sink; S4 §4 T7.2) and so an observation is obtainable. Open for the entries that follow: whether the session side is one archetype or one per program; which observation backs each side; the parameters. Hands to 9.10: the `gnome-shell`, `Xorg` and `pipewire` bindings in `c1-idle` and `c7-idle` rebind once the session entries exist; which of the five names a file carries stays 9.10's. No value changed by this entry.

## D2 — the session side is one archetype per program (2026-09-22)

Grounds: the three idle by different mechanisms, not at different scales of one. Not taken: one session-process archetype with the three bound to it, whose distribution would be centred between three mechanisms and describe none of them — the across-process fit the measurement audit found in `system-daemon` (scope card items 1–4, X8). ## D4 — the audio-server task is the PipeWire stack whole, one component per thread kind (2026-09-22)

By 인지오's decision, scope-card item 13: the audio-server archetype of D2 is observed on PipeWire as Ubuntu 24.04 ships it (PipeWire 1.0.5, WirePlumber 0.4.17; S4-13, S4-15), an identity binding under D3's rule, and its one task carries the three user services that make the audio server — `pipewire` (the media graph), `wireplumber` (the session manager) and `pipewire-pulse` (the PulseAudio-protocol server) — with each thread kind that wakes carried as its own component, the form 9.5 D16 gave measured applications. Grounds: the timelines state one task with the role of the audio server, and on this system that role is split across three processes that each hold part of it — the graph's one real-time data-loop thread, woken once per period by a timerfd and disarmed when the driver is not started (S2-18, S2-20); the session manager that suspends an idle node after 5 s (S2-19); the protocol server that PulseAudio-API clients talk to, which is the audio process the `scx_lavd` logs sample under Proton games while `pipewire` itself does not appear (S3-25). Per-thread components keep what matters to a scheduler — which thread wakes, at what rate, for how long — and keep the real-time data thread's table apart from the others'. Not taken: `pipewire` alone, which leaves out the process that suspends the graph and the one clients talk to; three tasks, one per process, which keeps the count of schedulable processes (9.8 D2's ground) but adds tasks to a role the timelines state as one — a timeline change that is 9.10's. Open: the observation (the audio stack with a null sink on the runner, no sound device existing there, S4-13, S4-15) and the states measured.
