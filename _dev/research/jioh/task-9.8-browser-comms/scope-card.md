- **T1 Chromium's process model and an idle renderer's behaviour.** How Chromium partitions a browsing session into processes (browser, renderer, GPU, utility, zygote), how renderer processes map to tabs, sites and frames on desktop Linux (site isolation, the soft process limit, process reuse), and what a renderer process does when its tab is idle, hidden or backgrounded (timer throttling, intensive wake-up throttling, tab freezing, discarding, memory-saver modes) as Chromium's documentation and source state it; any Linux observation of an idle renderer's wake cadence, CPU per wake and CPU share, per tab state; what Electron and CEF hosts share of this model.
- **T2 Chat and conferencing clients on Linux.** For Discord, Slack, Element, Zoom (and its helper processes) and Teams: process and thread structure on Linux, what the client does while idle (heartbeats, presence, websocket keep-alives, gateway pings, polling — the intervals their protocols or documentation state), what a message or call event costs, and any Linux observation of CPU share, wake cadence or CPU per wake, idle and under traffic; how each can be run without an account or against a self-hosted server.
- **T3 The Steam client behind a running game.** The Steam client's process structure on Linux (the client, its `steamwebhelper` CEF processes and their count, `gameoverlayui`), what it does while a game is running (overlay, friends and presence, chat, downloads), and any Linux observation of its CPU share, wake cadence or CPU per wake during play or idle.
- **T4 Shipped treatment of browsers, renderers and comms clients.** What process-treatment catalogues (ananicy rule sets, distribution defaults) assign to browsers, their renderer processes, Electron chat clients, Zoom and Steam; what the browsers themselves do to background tabs and their processes (priorities, cgroups, efficiency modes); any timing or rate value such a mechanism carries.
- **T5 Public traces and datasets.** Any public trace or dataset with per-process CPU or scheduling data of a Linux desktop browsing session with several renderer processes, of an Electron or CEF client at idle, or of a conferencing client (Perfetto or `perf` traces, bug reports with profiles of idle CPU use, system-monitor logs, benchmark databases), and what fields each carries.
- **T6 Browsers and comms clients as a scheduler workload.** How scheduler evaluations and benchmarks characterise a browser with many renderer processes, an Electron application or a chat client next to interactive work — as many mostly idle processes, as a wake-up source, as background load — with any reported CPU, run/wait or wake figures; what the literature says such a load does to interactivity and why.
- **T7 CI observability under a single-core pin.** Given a GitHub Actions hosted runner (4 vCPUs) on which the load is confined to one CPU with `taskset` while the instruments run on the other CPUs (the tooling is quoted in `input.md`): for a browser holding several loaded tabs (served locally), an Electron chat client (unauthenticated, or against a self-hosted Matrix homeserver or a local Discord-like server for message traffic), the Steam client, and a Zoom client, whether each can be installed and run there, driven into the state of interest (loaded tabs, backgrounded tabs, an idle client, a client receiving messages) without external accounts, and observed per process and per thread (`perf sched record` / `timehist` with wakeups, `/proc` snapshots, taskstats, process-role classification from command lines); how snap or Flatpak confinement of a browser affects `file://` access to host paths and what a locally served page changes; what the pin does to a multi-process tree; what the runner cannot observe (a service's real message traffic, a GPU, a display, a game beside the client).
