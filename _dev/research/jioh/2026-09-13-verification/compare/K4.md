## 2. What these claims ground in the dataset

### 2.1 `desktop-interactive.input_gap` (Part 2(a) rows 7–14)

**Stored object** (archetypes.yaml:108-116):
- `input_gap`: `{dist: lognormal-mixture, fluent_mean_us: 158000, fluent_sigma_log: 0.35, pause_probability: 0.34, pause_mean_us: 395235, pause_sigma_log: 0.6, overall_mean_us: 238660, sampling: per-iteration, source: "dhakal-chi18"}`
- `input_gap_family`: `{dist: family-declaration, family: two-component-lognormal, source: "roeser-rw24"}`

**Compiled use** (dataset/tools/wlc/sampling.py:53-60, :78-80):
- For every gap, a uniform draw chooses the pause component when it falls below `pause_probability`.
- The chosen component's `*_mean_us` is treated as a log-normal **mean** and turned into a median with `median = mean / exp(sigma²/2)` (line 59). A log-normal value is then drawn.
- Resulting medians: fluent 158,000 / e^0.06125 = **148,613 µs**; pause 395,235 / e^0.18 = **330,128 µs**.
- The analytic mean is (1−p)·fluent + p·pause = 0.66·158,000 + 0.34·395,235 = **238,660 µs**. That equals Dhakal's figure by construction, because `pause_mean_us` was solved for it.
- `overall_mean_us` itself is never read.

**Downstream.**
- The compiled wake events of every focused desktop-interactive task use these gaps.
- Burst CPU = uniform(0,1) × the preceding gap (burst_fraction, interbench:man-x). So the gap distribution sets both wake timing and foreground CPU demand. coreset-guide.md:146 reports 234 wake/run pairs and 29.8 s of CPU in `c1-office`'s writer over a 56 s focus window.
