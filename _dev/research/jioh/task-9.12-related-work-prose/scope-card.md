## Stage 2 topics

Neutral form, for the readers. Each is searched in the four classes of phase decision 4.

- **T1 Userspace scheduling delegation and runtime-loadable scheduling policy.** In Humphries et al., "ghOSt: Fast & Flexible User-Space Delegation of Linux Scheduling" (SOSP 2021): its stated motivation, mechanism and safety fallback. In the Linux kernel's `Documentation/scheduler/sched-ext.rst` (v6.12 and mainline): the kernel version it entered, what it says about safety, error handling and fallback, and whether it mentions the BPF verifier. In the sched-ext/scx repository: which schedulers it ships, what it says about production deployment and by whom, and any statement that a distribution or vendor ships a scx scheduler.
- **T2 scx_lavd's design and deployment.** What the scx_lavd documentation and source, Changwoo Min's talks (OSS NA 2024, LPC 2024, LPC 2025), and LWN's reports of them (lwn.net/Articles/991205, /1051430) say about how it estimates latency criticality, its origin in gaming, and who ships or deploys it (Steam Deck / SteamOS, Meta).
- **T3 Classical interactivity heuristics.** How the MLFQ chapter of Arpaci-Dusseau & Arpaci-Dusseau, *Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces*, and Corbató et al., "An Experimental Time-Sharing System" (SJCC 1962), describe demotion and the treatment of tasks that sleep; which shipped systems OSTEP says use MLFQ-like schedulers; what Stoica & Abdel-Wahab's EEVDF technical report (TR-95-22, 1995) and the kernel's `sched-eevdf.rst` / `sched-design-CFS.rst` say about sleeping tasks, lag and latency; whether Linux's default class is described as MLFQ, proportional share, or otherwise. Also, from the same texts: what they state about shortest-job-first's optimality and about giving lower priorities longer slices.
- **T4 Learned schedulers and their limits.** Mao et al., Decima (SIGCOMM 2019); Qiu et al., FIRM (OSDI 2020); Mao et al., Park (NeurIPS 2019): what each learns, from what inputs, its training requirements, and what each says about retraining across workloads or deployments.
- **T5 Recognize-then-route and LLM agents for kernel policy.** Wang et al., "Mixture-of-Schedulers: An Adaptive Scheduling Agent as a Learned Router for Expert Policies" (arXiv:2511.11628): how it recognizes workloads, what it trains and where, whether it reports recognition accuracy. Zheng et al., "Towards Agentic OS: An LLM Agent Framework for Linux Schedulers" (arXiv:2509.01245, all versions): its problem statement, architecture, profiling tools, workloads, headline speedups, research questions, what it says about RL-based schedulers, and its venue. Zheng et al., "Kgent" (eBPF '24). Lin et al., TuneAgent / OS-R1 (arXiv:2508.12551) and its venue. Jadhav et al. (arXiv:2506.02025) and arXiv:2511.11612. Liargkovas et al., TuxBot (arXiv:2605.15026); Dwivedula et al., Vulcan (arXiv:2512.25065). Any later work (2025–2026) that measures recognition or classification accuracy of an LLM reading a process list or system state, or that is a successor of SchedCP.
- **T6 Shipped game modes and how they recognize a game.** Microsoft's Game Mode documentation (Learn, Xbox Support) — how a game is recognized and what is done to other processes; Apple's documentation of Game Mode in macOS 14 and later — when it engages; Feral Interactive's GameMode (README, man page) — how a game requests or is put in the mode; whether any of them documents a list of executables, and whether any covers audio workstations.
- **T7 Declared scheduling classes and their uptake.** Linux `sched(7)`, `sched_setscheduler(2)`: the policies and who may set them. Apple's documentation of Quality-of-Service classes (names, who declares them). Whether common desktop background tools (`updatedb`/plocate/mlocate, tracker/LocalSearch, Baloo, backup and antivirus tools) ship with a declared idle or low class in their units or source; any survey or count of how many applications or units declare a scheduling class, nice level or I/O class; any documented case of applications declaring themselves higher priority than warranted.

Liu & Layland (JACM 1973): the conditions under which the deadline-driven algorithm is optimal. The kernel documentation of `SCHED_DEADLINE`, `SCHED_FIFO` and the default policy's algorithm in current mainline.
