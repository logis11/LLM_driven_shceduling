## Topics

- **T1 Userspace scheduling delegation and runtime-loadable scheduling policy.** In Humphries et al., "ghOSt: Fast & Flexible User-Space Delegation of Linux Scheduling" (SOSP 2021): its stated motivation, mechanism and safety fallback. In the Linux kernel's `Documentation/scheduler/sched-ext.rst` (at tag v6.12 and at mainline): the kernel version it entered, what it says about safety, error handling and fallback, and whether it mentions the BPF verifier. In the sched-ext/scx repository (github.com/sched-ext/scx, a release tag): which schedulers it ships, what it says about production deployment and by whom, and any statement that a distribution or vendor ships an scx scheduler.
- **T2 scx_lavd's design and deployment.** What the scx_lavd documentation and source, Changwoo Min's talks (Open Source Summit North America 2024; Linux Plumbers 2024 and 2025), and LWN's reports of them (lwn.net/Articles/991205, lwn.net/Articles/1051430) say about how scx_lavd estimates a task's latency criticality, its origin in gaming, and who ships or deploys it (Steam Deck / SteamOS, Meta, others), with dates.
- **T3 Classical interactivity heuristics.** How the MLFQ chapter of Arpaci-Dusseau & Arpaci-Dusseau, *Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces* (pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/OSTEP/cpu-sched-mlfq.pdf, and the SJF material in cpu-sched.pdf), and Corbató, Merwin-Daggett & Daley, "An Experimental Time-Sharing System" (AFIPS SJCC 1962), describe demotion and the treatment of tasks that sleep or wait for I/O; which shipped systems OSTEP says use MLFQ-like schedulers; what Stoica & Abdel-Wahab, "Earliest Eligible Virtual Deadline First" (Old Dominion TR-95-22, 1995) and the kernel's `Documentation/scheduler/sched-eevdf.rst` and `sched-design-CFS.rst` say about sleeping tasks, lag and latency; how Linux's default scheduling class is described (MLFQ, proportional share, or otherwise). From the same texts: what they state about shortest-job-first's optimality, and about giving lower-priority levels longer time slices.
- **T4 Learned schedulers and their limits.** Mao et al., "Learning Scheduling Algorithms for Data Processing Clusters" (Decima, SIGCOMM 2019); Qiu et al., "FIRM" (OSDI 2020); Mao et al., "Park: An Open Platform for Learning-Augmented Computer Systems" (NeurIPS 2019): what each learns, from what inputs, its training requirements, and what each says about retraining across workloads, clusters or deployments.
- **T5 Recognize-then-route and LLM agents for kernel policy.** Wang et al., "Mixture-of-Schedulers: An Adaptive Scheduling Agent as a Learned Router for Expert Policies" (arXiv:2511.11628): how it recognizes workloads, what it trains and where (per machine or once), what features it reads, whether it reports recognition accuracy, its venue. Zheng et al., "Towards Agentic OS: An LLM Agent Framework for Linux Schedulers" (arXiv:2509.01245, every version): its problem statement, architecture and profiling tools, its workloads and headline speedups, its research questions, what it says about RL-based or learned schedulers and which work it cites for that, and its venue. Zheng et al., "Kgent: Kernel Extensions Large Language Model Agent" (eBPF '24): what it produces from what input. Lin et al., "TuneAgent" / "OS-R1" (arXiv:2508.12551) and its venue. Jadhav et al. (arXiv:2506.02025) and arXiv:2511.11612: what each does with an LLM in HPC scheduling. Liargkovas et al., "TuxBot" (arXiv:2605.15026) and Dwivedula et al., "Vulcan" (arXiv:2512.25065): what each does. Any work of 2025–2026 that measures the recognition or classification accuracy of an LLM reading a process list or system state, or that succeeds the Agentic OS paper.
- **T6 Shipped game modes and how they recognize a game.** Microsoft's Game Mode documentation (Microsoft Learn, Xbox Support): how a game is recognized and what is done to other processes and to Windows Update; Apple's documentation of Game Mode in macOS 14 and later: when it engages and what it does; Feral Interactive's GameMode (github.com/FeralInteractive/gamemode README and man page): how a game requests or is put into the mode, and what it changes. Whether any of them documents a list of executables, an on/off state only or graded settings, and whether any covers audio workstations.
- **T7 Declared scheduling classes and their uptake.** Linux `sched(7)` and `sched_setscheduler(2)`: the policies, and who may set each. Apple's documentation of Quality-of-Service classes: their names and who declares them. Whether common desktop background tools (`updatedb` in plocate and mlocate, tracker/LocalSearch, Baloo, backup tools, ClamAV) ship with a declared idle or low CPU or I/O class in their systemd units or source. Any survey or count of how many applications, packages or units declare a scheduling policy, nice level or I/O class; any documented case of applications raising their own priority beyond what their work warrants.
- **T8 Name-keyed priority daemons.** Ananicy (github.com/Nefelim4ag/Ananicy) and ananicy-cpp (gitlab.com/ananicy-cpp/ananicy-cpp), and the CachyOS rules catalogue (github.com/CachyOS/ananicy-rules): what a rule matches on, what it sets, how entries are added and maintained.
- **T9 Proportional-share and real-time theory, as stated by the sources.** Waldspurger & Weihl, "Lottery Scheduling: Flexible Proportional-Share Resource Management" (OSDI 1994): what it says about starvation, proportional guarantees, and the cost of selecting a winner (list, tree). Liu & Layland, "Scheduling Algorithms for Multiprogramming in a Hard-Real-Time Environment" (JACM 1973): the conditions under which the deadline-driven algorithm is optimal. The kernel documentation of `SCHED_DEADLINE` (`sched-deadline.rst`), `SCHED_FIFO`/`SCHED_RR` and the default policy's algorithm at current mainline.
- **T10 Kernel scheduling quantities.** Published values, from measurement or documentation, for: the cost of one scheduling decision (pick-next-task) in Linux; the cost of a context switch (direct cost and with cache effects) on current x86 hardware; how many context switches or scheduling events per second per core a desktop or a server sees; typical time-slice lengths in shipped schedulers. With machine, kernel version and method named.
- **T11 Local LLM inference latency, determinism, constrained decoding, reasoning-first output.** Published measurements of end-to-end latency for a short structured output (tens to a few hundred tokens) from 3–8-billion-parameter quantized models on consumer CPUs or GPUs (llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM), and of hosted-API latency for comparable outputs. Documentation of grammar-constrained or guided decoding (llama.cpp GBNF, vLLM guided/structured decoding, Ollama structured outputs) and what it guarantees about the output. Studies of output non-determinism at temperature 0 with a fixed seed. Studies on whether eliciting reasoning before a final answer improves the answer (chain-of-thought), and its latency cost.
- **T12 Desktop session and activity durations.** Any published measurement of how long PC gaming sessions last, and how often the set of running applications on a desktop changes per hour.
- **T13 Media deadlines.** Documented buffer sizes and sample rates for low-latency audio production (JACK, PipeWire, common DAW defaults) and the period they imply; documented display refresh rates and frame budgets for PC games.
- **T14 Benchmark-vendor history.** Primary or contemporaneous reports of the 2011 departures of AMD, Nvidia and VIA from BAPCo, and the reasons each gave.
- **T15 CI observability.** Whether a GitHub Actions hosted runner can observe T10's quantities on its own kernel — context switches per second (`/proc/stat`, `vmstat`), context-switch cost by a pipe ping-pong between two pinned processes, scheduling latency (`perf sched`, `/proc/schedstat`) — and T11's latency for a small quantized model on its CPU (llama.cpp with a 1–8B GGUF model, short JSON output).

## Record format

Each reader writes `search/S<k>-<class>.md`:

1. **Search log** — one row per query: date, engine or venue, exact query terms, hits followed, dead ends (with the HTTP status).
2. **Candidates** — per candidate: a provisional id `S<k>-NN`; full citation; copy read (URL or commit, version, date accessed, local path under `sources/<id>/`, SHA-256 of the file fetched); verbatim passages with locators (page, section, figure, slide, `file:line` at a commit); coverage per topic T1–T15 — covers (naming the object, unit, statistic, scope and population) or does not cover; whether it is one observation (one population, trace or run) and whether machine, subject and window are named.
3. **Not found** — each topic with no candidate, with the searches that established it.

Source copies go to `sources/<id>/` under the task folder (gitignored; they do not survive the clone). The record must be self-sufficient: everything a reader needs is in the quoted passages and the copy identification, never "see the local file". Verbatim means verbatim; a passage that cannot be quoted is not a passage.
